Ask small businesses why a software project stalled and you hear the same two answers: it cost too much, and it was too complicated to get running. In the UK government's 2025 survey of 2,000 SMEs, the most-cited barrier to adopting new software was that it "was too expensive for the business" (32%), and the next three were all forms of complexity: no time to explore the options (21%), doubt that it would pay off (14%), and software "too complicated for my business to adopt" (12%) ([Ipsos for the Department for Business and Trade, 2025](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/688a438aff8c05468cb7b0f0/sme_tech_adoption_dbt_report.pdf)). ArrivePing was built to take both barriers off the table: a 30-day free trial with as many users as you like, no hardware, no contract and nothing to buy up front, with an AI setup agent that builds your workspace in under an hour and a library of short how-to videos so your team is working on its own within a day or two.

## What stops small businesses from adopting software?

The two barriers show up in every serious survey, and they compound each other. Cost is the obvious one. Ipsos asked UK SMEs what held them back and a third said "software was too expensive for the business" (32%). Complexity is the quieter one, because it hides behind several answers at once: 21% said "I do not have time/capacity to explore the options available", 14% said "we are not convinced that adopting new technology would be beneficial", 12% said the software "is too complicated for my business to adopt", and another 12% said "our employees will not know how to utilise new technology effectively" ([Ipsos/DBT, 2025](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/688a438aff8c05468cb7b0f0/sme_tech_adoption_dbt_report.pdf), Figure 5.4). Add those up and more owners are blocked by the effort of adopting than by the price tag.

The cost of getting it wrong is real. Capterra's 2026 Software Buying Trends report, based on more than 3,300 software buyers, found that "two-thirds of today's software buyers experience disruptions during implementation, purchase regret, or both", and that "89% of software buyers who regret their purchase experienced implementation disruptions". By Capterra's definition, "only one in three software buyers is a successful software adopter" ([Capterra, 2025](https://www.capterra.com/resources/software-buying-trends-2026/)). Regret, in other words, is mostly a rollout problem, not a features problem.

None of this means small businesses are avoiding technology. The U.S. Chamber of Commerce's 2026 Empowering Small Business report found 41% of small businesses now run six or more technology platforms, up nine points in a year, and 84% plan to increase their technology use over the next two to three years ([U.S. Chamber of Commerce, 2026](https://www.uschamber.com/technology/2026-empowering-small-business-report)). The appetite is there. The barriers are what to buy, what it will really cost, and whether the crew will use it.

## What does "removing the cost barrier" actually mean?

Cost isn't just the subscription. For field service software it usually includes vehicle GPS hardware, a setup fee, an annual contract you can't leave, and the hours a manager spends getting it configured. Complexity is the barrier that regret surveys keep pointing at, and it has three parts: the time to set the system up, the time to learn it, and the fear of being stuck with nobody to ask. ArrivePing answers each one directly.

## Playbook: how to evaluate software without hitting either barrier

1. **Write down the full cost before the demo.** Subscription, hardware, setup fee, contract length and the hours your own people will spend. Capterra's finding that regret follows implementation trouble means the last line is the one to watch.
2. **Insist on a trial with your real team on it.** A trial limited to two users tells you nothing about how dispatch and the crew will use it. Ask for unlimited users during the trial, and a card that isn't charged.
3. **Time the setup yourself.** Start a clock at sign-up and stop it when the first real job is dispatched. If the answer is weeks, that's the complexity barrier in hours.
4. **Watch the tutorials before you commit.** If a vendor's how-to videos are long, vague or missing, the product is probably hard to learn. A library of one-minute videos usually means the workflows are simple enough to fit in one.
5. **Check how you leave.** Month-to-month billing and a full export (CSV, Excel, PDF, JSON) mean a wrong choice costs one month, not a year. See [what field service software really costs](/blog/field-service-software-cost) and [why base hits beat home-run software](/blog/base-hit-software-small-business).
6. **Give it two days, then decide.** Run the sample day on day one, a real day on day two, and ask the crew. If they're using it without being told to, you've cleared both barriers.
