Artificial intelligence (AI) is everywhere in field service management. From smarter scheduling to predictive maintenance, the promise of AI is enticing. But here’s the hard truth: relying on AI alone won’t grow your trade business. Sustainable growth comes from combining AI with human-centered workflows, local expertise, and clear communication strategies.

In this post, we’ll explore what the data says about AI in field service, the hidden costs of missed opportunities, and how you can combine technology with human insight to achieve real, long-term growth.

## The State of AI in Field Service: Impressive—but Not Enough

AI adoption is climbing fast:

- Nearly **80% of high-performing FSM organizations use AI**, compared to just 59% of underperformers (Praxedo).
- The field service market is booming, projected to grow from **US $4.55 billion in 2024** to **US $5.12 billion in 2025** (The Business Research Company).

Yet, despite this growth, technology on its own isn’t the driver. Companies that thrive blend AI with smart workflows, strong communication, and local market awareness.

## The Hidden Danger: Missed Calls Cost More Than You Think

While AI optimizes back-end processes, the real revenue killer often goes unnoticed: **missed customer calls**.

- Home services businesses miss **27% of calls**, wasting thousands in monthly revenue (Invoca).
- Across industries, **62% of calls go unanswered**, and **85% of callers never call back** (Dialzara).
- Small businesses lose between **$100–$1,200 per missed call**—potentially **$12,600/month** (Dialzara).

Even 10 missed calls a week, at $100 profit each, equals **$52,000 in lost annual revenue** (IPFone).

**Insight**: AI can’t replace the importance of direct customer contact. Technology must work hand-in-hand with real-time communication.

## Why AI Alone Falls Short

- **Human Context Matters**: AI can help plan the day, but empathy, judgment, and trade-specific expertise can’t be automated.
- **Workforce Crisis**: Half of today’s field technicians don’t see a lifelong career in trades (TechRadar). AI can supplement training, but it can’t replace decades of knowledge.
- **Preserving Knowledge**: Experts retiring means wisdom lost—unless businesses use AI as a tool to capture and pass on field experience.
